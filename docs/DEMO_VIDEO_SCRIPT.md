# Prialto Finance App — Demo Video Script (v2)

Full narration script for a walkthrough video, organized by screen/segment so
it can be recorded section-by-section and stitched together. Approximate
total runtime at a natural pace: **10–12 minutes** — longer than the first
cut because there's genuinely more to show now: a full black-and-orange
redesign, our real delivery-center names, a new Capacity & Staffing page, and
an interactive variance view, on top of everything that was already here.

**Voice direction:** This is narrated in George's voice, but it should never
sound like narration. It's Akhil talking — a senior financial analyst at
Prialto explaining something he built himself, to people he works with every
day. Read it like you're walking a colleague through your own screen, not
like you're reading copy. Let sentences breathe where there's a period. Lean
into the moments that sound like an actual person thinking out loud ("here's
the thing," "and this is the part I actually care about") — those aren't
filler, they're where the personality lives. Avoid the flat, evenly-paced
cadence that reads as AI-generated; vary pace, let the important lines land
slower than the connective ones.

Wherever the script says "demo data" or "mock data," keep that phrasing in
the video — the app is running in preview mode on generated numbers, not
real Prialto financials, and the narration should never let that ambiguity
slip.

## 1. Cold open (0:00–0:25)

**On screen:** App loads into the Revenue & Unit Economics dashboard on the
new black-and-orange theme. Let it sit for a beat before speaking — the
color alone should register before a word is said.

**Narration:**

> "Okay — so this is something I built. I've always had a passion for
> finding ways to improve how we operate here at Prialto, and I think this
> could be a real boost for the company — I sat down and built the finance
> app I've been wishing we had. It's called Prialto Finance, and it's the
> one place that pulls together everything leadership and department heads
> need to actually understand the business: revenue, delivery, staffing,
> the full financial picture, and how any of it moved over time — instead
> of me pulling all of that together by hand into a deck the night before
> every meeting. Let me show you what's in here."

---

## 2. Revenue & Unit Economics — top-line KPIs (0:25–1:05)

**On screen:** Top of the Revenue page. Point at MRR, ARR, Active Units, Net
Revenue Retention.

**Narration:**

> "This is where I'd start every finance conversation, and it's where the
> app starts too. Monthly recurring revenue, annual run-rate, active
> units — that's our Prialto Unit count, 55 hours a month each — and net
> revenue retention, which tells you whether our existing base is growing
> or shrinking on its own, completely separate from new sales. Every tile
> with a trend shows the move versus the prior period, and it's color-coded
> so you read good-or-bad in about half a second, not by parsing a plus or
> minus sign."

---

## 3. New metrics — ARPU, churn, new units (1:05–1:50)

**On screen:** Second row of stat tiles: ARPU, Net New Units, Churn Rate,
New Units Added.

**Narration:**

> "Below that — a row I added because 'MRR is up' is not, on its own, an
> answer. ARPU, average revenue per client, tells you whether we're
> growing by landing bigger accounts or just piling up more small ones.
> Net New Units nets out the whole month in one number — everything added
> minus everything lost. And churn rate sits right next to new units
> added, monthly, unit-based, so growth and attrition are in the same
> sentence instead of two different reports that never quite get read
> together."

## 4. MRR movement bridge (1:50–2:25)

**On screen:** MRR Movement bar chart — New, Expansion, Contraction, Churn.

**Narration:**

> "This is the chart I'd pull up first in any leadership review. It's the

> new business, how much from existing clients expanding, and what we lost
> to contraction and churn. It answers the question people actually ask,
> which isn't 'did MRR move' — it's 'why.'"

## 7. Utilization & Delivery Cost (3:40–4:30)

**On screen:** Navigate to Utilization & Delivery Cost. Show the stat tiles,
utilization trend, cost-per-hour-by-center list, the billed-vs-delivered
chart, at-risk clients table.

**Narration:**

> "This page is about delivery, not sales — hours billed against hours
> actually delivered, straight from Time Doctor. The gap between billed
> and delivered is hours a client is paying for that we haven't gotten to
> yet, and depending on the trend, that's either a catch-up opportunity or
> an early churn signal. We break cost-per-delivered-hour out by our three
> delivery centers — GT, PH, and KE, Guatemala, the Philippines, and
> Kenya — that's how we actually refer to them day to day internally, and
> now the app does too. And at the bottom, a live, sorted list of the
> clients with the lowest utilization right now. That's account
> management's early-warning list, and it updates before a client ever
> has to become a churn conversation."

## 8. Capacity & Staffing — new page (4:30–5:45)

**On screen:** Navigate to the new Capacity & Staffing page. Show the four
top tiles, then the headcount-by-center chart, training pipeline, and the
hiring-vs-unit-growth chart.

**Narration:**

> "This page didn't exist until this round, and honestly, it might be my
> favorite one in the app now. Here's the thing — utilization tells you
> whether we're delivering what we billed. It doesn't tell you whether
> we'll still be able to say that in three months. This page does.
>
> Active assistants, bench coverage — that's certified people who are
> trained and ready but not yet assigned, our buffer against attrition and
> leave — and how many people are moving through our roughly four-week
> training pipeline right now, broken out stage by stage: orientation,
> core skills, live shadowing, certification. Attrition rate, by center,
> because GT, PH, and KE don't move the same way and shouldn't be averaged
> into one number that hides which one actually needs attention. Client
> backup coverage — the percentage of active units that have a named
> backup assistant on file, which is what keeps one person's sick day from
> ever touching a client. And this chart right here — hiring against unit
> growth, side by side, every month. That's the one that tells you whether
> we're staffing ahead of demand or scrambling to catch up to it. If
> you're only going to remember one new page from this walkthrough, make
> it this one — it's the earliest warning we have for a staffing gap,
> weeks before it would ever show up as a delivery problem."

## 9. Variance — month-over-month, on your terms (5:45–6:50)

**On screen:** Navigate to the new Variance page. Demonstrate changing the
baseline and comparison month dropdowns live — show it recompute instantly.
Click "Compare to same month last year" once to show that path too.

**Narration:**

> "This one I built because I was tired of variance analysis living in a
> spreadsheet that only ever compared whatever two months someone happened
> to set up six months ago. Watch this — these are two live dropdowns.
> Baseline period, comparison period. Default is last month against this
> month, standard MoM. But say someone in the room asks 'how does this
> compare to a year ago' —" *(click the year-over-year button)* "— one
> click, done. Or pick any two months at all, a specific budget month, a
> board-meeting month, whatever the conversation actually needs. Every key
> metric recalculates instantly — MRR, margins, utilization, cash,
> headcount, DSO, ARPU — with the direction of 'good' built in per metric,
> so DSO going down is green and revenue going down is red, not the other
> way around by accident. Nobody has to go build this in a spreadsheet and
> email it back. It's just here."

## 10. Core Financials (6:50–7:55)

**On screen:** Navigate to Core Financials. Show gross/operating margin,
cash balance, cash runway, then the new second tile row — EBITDA margin,
Rule of 40, DSO, revenue per employee — then revenue and EBITDA trend
charts, AR aging, opex by department, P&L and balance sheet tables.

**Narration:**

> "Core Financials is the page I'd expect our controller to open first —
> gross margin, operating margin, cash balance, cash runway, up top,
> where they belong. And I expanded this page this round, because a
> leadership team doesn't stop at margin. EBITDA margin. Rule of 40 —
> revenue growth plus EBITDA margin in one score, the number growth-stage
> companies actually get measured against. Days sales outstanding, so we
> know how fast we're really collecting on what we've billed, not just how
> much is overdue. Revenue per employee, a straight capital-efficiency
> check as headcount scales. And a burn multiple that just reads 'n/a'
> when we're cash-flow positive — which, itself, is the answer.
>
> Below that — revenue and EBITDA trend, AR aging, opex by department, and
> full P&L and balance sheet tables, built to read from QuickBooks' current
> accounting API rather than the legacy reports API that's getting retired
> next year. So this doesn't quietly break the day that happens."

## 12. How the integrations actually work (8:30–9:35)

**On screen:** Stay on Data Health as a backdrop, or a simple architecture
slide if one exists.

**Narration:**

> "So how does this actually plug into what we already use? Four sources,
> four jobs. Salesforce feeds bookings and pipeline. QuickBooks feeds
> realized revenue and the entire Core Financials page — and we built this
> against QuickBooks' current, documented accounting API on purpose, not
> the older reports API, because that one's being phased out and I didn't
> want to build something that breaks itself on a deadline we don't
> control. Time Doctor feeds actual hours worked — that's Utilization and
> half of Capacity & Staffing right there. And Domo is the flexible one —
> it can pull from dashboards we already have there, or push data from
> this app back into Domo for teams who are used to living in it.
>
> Every one of those connects the same way — a secure sign-in, done once
> by an admin, and after that it syncs on its own schedule. The moment
> that happens, every dashboard I just walked you through stops being demo
> data and starts being real Prialto numbers, without anything else about
> how it looks or works changing at all."

---

## 13. Where this is headed / close (9:35–10:45)

**On screen:** Back to the Revenue dashboard, black-and-orange theme filling
the frame.

**Narration:**

> "Here's why I actually built this, and I want to say it plainly: we're
> trying to scale fast, and the faster we scale, the more expensive it
> gets to make a big call on stale information. Real-time actuals aren't a
> nice-to-have at our next stage — they're the infrastructure critical
> decisions get made on top of. Do we open a fourth center. Is attrition
> in one region actually a problem or just noise. Is a client's slipping
> utilization worth an intervention call today. Those calls get faster,
> and better, the moment everyone in the room is looking at the same
> current numbers instead of three spreadsheets that don't quite agree.
> That's what this is actually for.
>
> To be straight with you about where we are: it's live, anyone with the
> link can open it today, because company sign-in isn't switched on yet —
> that's next. The numbers you've been looking at are demo data, shaped to
> look like our real economics, not the real thing yet. What's left is
> connecting the four systems for real and turning on Google or Microsoft
> sign-in so it's locked to Prialto. After that, this stops being
> something I built and becomes the place we all just check.
>
> Take a look, click around — and tell me what you'd want to see before we
> flip it over to live data."

## Notes for whoever records/edits this

- Every dashboard is fully interactive in the actual recording — hover
  charts to show real tooltips, and linger on at least one tooltip per
  chart type (line, bar, donut) so viewers see it's not a static image.
- Section 9 (Variance) needs an actual live dropdown interaction captured,
  not a static screenshot — the whole point of that page is that it
  recomputes instantly, and the video should prove it.
- The preview-mode warning banner ("SSO isn't configured yet...") is
  visible on every page against the new dark theme — don't crop it out.
  It's part of being upfront about current state, and section 13
  references it directly.
- If recording in segments, capture 2–3 extra seconds of static footage
  before and after each interaction (hover, click, scroll, dropdown
  change) so cuts don't feel abrupt.
- Suggested on-screen lower-third captions per section: "Revenue & Unit
  Economics," "New: ARPU, Churn & Daily Activity," "Utilization & Delivery
  Cost," "New: Capacity & Staffing," "New: Variance — Any Two Periods,"
  "Core Financials," "Data Health," "How It Connects," "Where This Is
  Headed."
- Delivery note for whoever reads this in George's voice: the two lines
  most worth re-taking until they land right are the Capacity & Staffing
  opener ("honestly, it might be my favorite page in the app now") and the
  closing paragraph starting "Here's why I actually built this" — those
  are the moments the whole video is building toward, and they should
  sound like a real conviction, not a script wrap-up.
